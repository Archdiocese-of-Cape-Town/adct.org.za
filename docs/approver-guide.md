# Approving an event

This guide is for a dean, vice-dean or archdiocese reviewer who decides
whether a parish's event goes on the public events page.

You need a WordPress account and an email address. That is all.

## The short version

| Stage | What you see | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| An event arrives | An email with **Approve**, **Reject** and **Edit** links | Open the **Approve** link |
| The link opens | A page showing the event, with buttons | Check it, then press the button |
| The button works | The page confirms your decision | Nothing more to do |
| The event is live | It is on the events page and in the calendar | Nothing more to do |

**Opening a link decides nothing.** The link only shows you the page. A
decision is made when you press a button on that page. This is on purpose: it
means a virus scanner or a mail system that follows links cannot approve
anything on your behalf.

## Approving from an email

1. Open the email from the archdiocese. It lists each event with an
   **Approve**, **Reject** and **Edit** link beside it.
2. Click **Approve**. The page opens and shows the event's title, date, time
   and place.
3. Check those four things against the notice the parish sent:
   - Is the date right?
   - Is the start time right?
   - Is the place right, and the right parish?
   - Is this an event, rather than Mass times, the collection list or the
     sick list?
4. If it is all correct, press **Approve**. If it is not, press **Edit**.

## Correcting an event before approving it

**Edit** opens a form with the title, date, time and description. You can
change any of them and press **Save**. Saving does **not** publish the event;
you still have to approve it afterwards.

You can change:

- the title
- the date
- the start time
- the description

You cannot change the parish or the venue from an email. If either is wrong,
press **Reject** and say what it should be. An archdiocese reviewer will fix
it and publish it.

A good correction is small. Fix the date and time, leave the parish's own
wording alone.

## Rejecting an event

Press **Reject** and give a short reason for the parish, for example
"The date should be 19 October, not 12." The parish sees the reason and can
send a corrected email. A rejection is not a black mark; sending it back is
the normal way to fix a mistake.

## Problems and what to do about them

| Problem | What to do |
|---|---|
| The link says it has expired | Links last 14 days. Ask the archdiocese to send the event again, or approve it from **Parish Intake → Review queue**. |
| The link says it has already been used | Each link works once. Somebody else may already have decided. The page tells you who and when. |
| The page shows no buttons | You are no longer the approver for that parish. Ask the archdiocese. |
| The date is wrong | Press **Edit**, correct the date, save, then approve. |
| The parish or venue is wrong | Press **Reject** with the correct parish or hall. |
| The event is really Mass times or a collection list | Press **Reject**. This should be rare; telling the archdiocese helps them fix the settings. |
| Two events look like the same one | Reject the duplicate with a note, and approve the better one. |
| You approved something by mistake | Ask the website administrator to revert it. It cannot be undone from an email. |
| You approved it but it is not on the events page | Ask the website administrator to look at **Parish Intake → Health**. The decision was recorded; publishing it separately may have failed. |
| No email arrived at all | Check the spam folder, then check **Parish Intake → Review queue** in WordPress. |
| You want one email a day instead of one per event | Switch **daily digest** to **each event**, or the other way round, on your WordPress profile page. |

## Digests instead of one email per event

If you chose **daily digest**, you get one email a day listing everything
waiting, not one email per event. Digests are sent in the morning, so an
event that arrives at 14:00 is usually in that evening's digest rather than
being mailed straight away.

The choice is on your WordPress profile page. A deanery assignment can set the
preference for the dean.

## How this is kept safe

These are the rules the buttons follow. You do not have to know them to
approve an event, but they explain what you may see.

- **A link only shows. A button acts.** Nothing is approved by opening a
  link.
- **A link works once.** After you use it, it cannot be reused, even by you.
- **The first decision counts.** If you and somebody else both open the page,
  whoever presses a button first is recorded, and the other person is told who
  decided and when.
- **A link stops working after 14 days.** A used or expired link leaves the
  event in the queue for the next person.
- **Your access is checked again every time you open or press something.**
  If a deanery reassignment has removed your approval for that parish since
  the email was sent, the buttons disappear and a later press is refused. The
  event stays in the queue for whoever can decide it.

## Approving inside WordPress instead

If you have a WordPress login, you can work without email at all.

1. Open **Review queue** in the admin menu. A dean who is not an archdiocese
   reviewer sees **Review queue** as its own top-level menu.
2. Choose **Awaiting approval** to see everything waiting for you.
3. Tick up to 25 events and approve or reject them together.
4. Open one event first if you want to check it in detail.

The WordPress queue is always open. The email link is the short way in.
